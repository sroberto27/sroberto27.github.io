# Candidate HCI and immersive-evaluation framework

This framework organizes future research. Research questions and methods must be narrowed into a specific approved protocol before participant recruitment or data collection.

## Staged evaluation program

1. **Technical benchmark and acceptance testing:** verify data integrity, optics, transforms, provider states, performance, recovery and exports. This produces technical evidence only.
2. **Expert formative review:** location scouts, filmmakers, cinematographers or relevant HCI/VR experts examine workflow fit, terminology, evidence gaps and safety-critical misunderstandings. Obtain the applicable ethics determination before involving people as research participants.
3. **Task-based usability study:** participants complete standardized discovery, immersive inspection, comparison and shot-planning tasks. Capture effectiveness, efficiency, errors, recovery, workload/usability measures and qualitative explanation.
4. **Controlled comparison:** compare SLiVR with a clearly defined conventional or component-separated workflow. Use equivalent materials, counterbalance order when appropriate, control training and record carryover/learning effects.
5. **Remote-to-site validation:** compare remote judgments with current on-site observations or authoritative measurements to study what immersive scouting can and cannot establish.
6. **Longitudinal field study:** after reliability and governance mature, examine adoption, collaboration, evolving evidence and real project decisions over time.
7. **Instrumented Treedis/WebXR study:** after Phase 6 authorization and validation, align protocol-approved head/controller/input trajectories and Treedis context with task outcomes. Use the minimum necessary event families, report loss/overhead and treat head direction as a proxy rather than eye tracking or attention.

Do not begin with all stages at once. Select the smallest design that answers a clear research question and uses the prototype version actually available.

## Core task family

- Find locations matching a scene brief and explain rejected alternatives.
- Inspect a current Treedis location and identify visual opportunities, coverage gaps and on-site questions.
- Compare candidates while preserving unknowns; choose a preferred option and backup with evidence.
- Reopen the saved decision after a delay or handoff.
- Build a basic camera/actor/path arrangement from the supplied downtown scenario and create a variant.
- Recover from a defined provider or save failure and export the required evidence packet.

Task keys and scoring rubrics must distinguish factual correctness, evidence completeness, spatial understanding, creative judgment and application-operation skill.

## Study-design controls

- Define participant expertise, inclusion/exclusion and recruitment source before analysis.
- Use the same location/capture versions and task materials across comparison conditions.
- Counterbalance condition/order when learning or fatigue can affect results.
- Provide equivalent training and record prior experience with scouting, maps, 360/VR and shot-design tools.
- Separate facilitator help, software failure, provider failure and participant error.
- Predefine timeouts, exclusions, missing-data treatment, outcome derivations and confirmatory versus exploratory analyses.
- For expert rubrics or qualitative coding, document rater training, independence and agreement.
- Report effect estimates and uncertainty alongside statistical tests; retain null and contradictory results.

## Reproducibility package for a future paper

Preserve the protocol, artifact/build identifier, feature register snapshot, catalog/capture manifest, task materials, training script, condition configuration, measurement dictionary, questionnaires and scoring instructions, deidentified analysis dataset where permitted, transformation/analysis scripts, exclusion log, results tables and limitations. Never publish provider-restricted media or identifiable/private production data.
